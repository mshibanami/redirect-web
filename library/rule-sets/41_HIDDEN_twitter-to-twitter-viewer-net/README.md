# Twitter to Twitter Viewer (twitterviewer.net)

## 2026/09/22

This rule is now hidden because the website is no longer considered safe/trustworthy, although they still offer their service as a Twitter alternative, for the following reasons/smells:

* They started showing ads from suspicious ad networks with a history of malicious/phishing activity, such as Fortinet or Chong Lua Dao, according to [Gridinsoft](https://gridinsoft.com/online-virus-scanner/url/twitterviewer-net#blocklist).
* The owner of this service is questionable. As of now, two people who claim to have made this service have been confirmed: Evan Mercer and James Mitchell. Evan claims that this is a one-person project. (Details: [dev.to](https://dev.to/evanmercerdev/show-dev-read-public-x-profiles-threads-and-media-without-an-account-6di)). On the other hand, James is the first person who claimed that he created it at the very beginning of the release of this service. (Details: [twitterviewer.net](https://twitterviewer.net/blog/twitter-transformation-developer-perspective)) It's hard to believe that James simply changed his name on the internet since James posted [an official article](https://twitterviewer.net/blog/nitter-alternatives-compared) after Evan claimed that he was the only person behind it. It might be just that they want to keep it anonymous, but since Evan emphasized that he is one person, it could be a sign that the service is managed by a group pretending to be an individual, which is one of the smells.
* On 14th August, Evan posted an article and claimed that "No signup, no login, no payment. There's also no account system on my side." (Details: [dev.to](https://dev.to/evanmercerdev/show-dev-read-public-x-profiles-threads-and-media-without-an-account-6di)) However, this service already had an account system even before they posted this article, which makes it look like they intentionally attract users with misinformation.

As of now, technically, it should be okay to browse this service with an ad blocker and never log in. But there are already enough reasons to hide this rule for our Library.
